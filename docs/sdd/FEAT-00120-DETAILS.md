All changes are in `tt-data-league-frontend`. There is no API change: every view uses the data the page already loads. The approved mockup is https://claude.ai/artifact/CVjNnPuHjyjsDbbrkqSJ1m. Steps 1-3 and 5-12 were delivered in the first pass and steps 4 and 13-20 in the grouped week view pass (see `# Notes`). Steps 21-28 are the layout change requested after that: the detail panel moves below the calendar and the calendar fills the horizontal space. Steps 29-38 are the next change: the detail panel is removed and a match is opened in a dialog. Steps 13-20 are kept for history and must not be redone; where steps 21-24 contradict the side-by-side layout of the approved mockup or of steps 15, 17 and 20, steps 21-24 win; where steps 29-38 contradict anything about the detail panel (steps 5, 7, 8, 9, 21, 23, 24 and the panel parts of the tests, README and validation steps), steps 29-38 win and the panel is gone. # Implementation Guidelines
- Follow `tt-data-league-frontend/AGENTS.md` and `docs/frontend/design-contract.md`. The mockup's hex values stand for the tokens in `src/index.css`: use the variables, not the hex values.
- The mockup is the visual reference, and its sample data and non-working controls are not requirements. Day, jornada and tablet layouts were not drawn: they keep their current structure and take the new entries, panel, filters and legend.
- Counting states per day is presentation only. The frontend still does not compute calendar states, deadlines or the current round.
- FullCalendar stays for the day and jornada list views only. The month view (`MonthGrid`) and the week view (`WeekGrid`) are separate components: `dateClick` is mouse-only and cells must be keyboard accessible, and FullCalendar cannot render nested collapsible groups of native buttons inside an event.
- Grouping in the week view is presentation only: it uses `match.source` (or the selected source when the response carries none), `match.competition` and the `calendarState` the API returned. The frontend still does not compute states, deadlines or the current round.
- The week view's three levels are day > source > competition > matches. Expansion state is local to the component, is not stored in the URL or in browser storage, and starts collapsed whenever the week or a filter changes. Collapsing a source collapses its competitions.
- Keep all URL parameters and their meanings, so existing links keep working. `view=month` remains valid.
- Detail dialog (scope change of 2026-11-05, supersedes the panel rules of the next bullet): there is no detail panel, no sort control and no day selection state. Selecting a match entry (week, day, jornada, undated list) opens a modal dialog; selecting a month cell or a week day header opens the day view of that date. The dialog's open state is local component state, not in the URL. The dialog shows only data already in the calendar response and never calls the match details endpoint. Its content is what the old detail row showed plus the link to the match summary page.
- Layout (scope change of 2026-11-05): the detail panel is always below the calendar, in all four views and at every width; there is no side-by-side layout and no breakpoint that restores one. The panel has no scroll area of its own. The calendar uses the full width of the content area; only the `/calendari` route lifts the shared 1360px content cap, and no other page changes width. Selecting a day or a match scrolls the panel into view; nothing else scrolls the page.
- Out of scope: backend, REST, GraphQL or MCP changes; a jornada view across sources; arrow-key navigation in the month grid; the pipeline orchestrator calendar; grouping by source or competition in the day, jornada and month views and in the detail panel; persisting the expansion state; an "expand all" or "collapse all" control. Also out of scope: a sticky or floating panel, a user-switchable side-by-side layout, resizable panes, and changing the width of any page other than `/calendari`. Also out of scope for the dialog: the full acta (games, sets, lineups) and any call to the match details endpoint, next and previous match navigation inside the dialog, a deep link or URL parameter that reopens the dialog, and a way to sort matches.
